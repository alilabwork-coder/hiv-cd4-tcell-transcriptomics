# Host transcriptional signatures of progressive HIV infection in purified CD4+ T cells

## Overview

This project is a reproducible exploratory analysis of host gene-expression patterns in purified CD4+ T cells from people with untreated HIV infection and HIV-uninfected controls.

The primary goal is to identify transcriptional signatures associated with progressive HIV infection and interpret their potential relationship to interferon signaling, immune activation, T-cell biology, and HIV pathogenesis.

## Research question

In purified CD4+ T cells, which host transcriptional changes distinguish untreated acute and chronic progressive HIV infection from non-progressors and HIV-uninfected controls?

## Dataset

- **Source:** NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO)
- **Series accession:** GSE6740
- **Dataset record:** GDS2649
- **Platform:** Affymetrix Human Genome U133A Array (GPL96)
- **Organism:** Homo sapiens
- **Study design:** 40 microarray samples from 20 male participants; paired CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell samples were collected from each participant.
- **Groups:** Acute HIV infection, chronic progressive HIV infection, HIV non-progressors with low/undetectable viral load, and HIV-uninfected controls.
- **Treatment status:** HIV-positive participants were untreated before study entry.

The source study examined ex vivo CD4+ and CD8+ T cells from untreated people with HIV at distinct clinical stages and progression phenotypes.

## Analysis plan

The initial analysis will include CD4+ T-cell samples only.

- Progressive HIV group: acute HIV + chronic progressive HIV
- Reference group: HIV non-progressors + HIV-uninfected controls
- Metadata quality control and sample-group verification
- Exploratory data analysis and principal component analysis
- Differential-expression analysis
- Pathway and gene-set interpretation
- Visualization of interferon-stimulated and HIV-relevant host genes
- Clear reporting of limitations and non-clinical intended use

## Important limitations

This is an educational and hypothesis-generating reanalysis of a small, historical public microarray cohort. Results will not be presented as diagnostic, prognostic, causal, or clinically validated. Findings require confirmation in independent and contemporary cohorts.
